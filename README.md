# Culturally Competent Social Robotics during Navigation
Cultural competence has become important in social robotics because researchers discovered that influences preferences, expectations and engagement during human-robot interaction.
Cultural Competence has been defined as the ability of a system to understand and adapt to different cultural contexts.

However, since the concept of culture is fuzzy, with hundreds of definitions, is not easy to understand how to implement such a feature.
From general knowledge and qualitative studies, we know that it's easier to interact with a new culture if we have previous knowledge. 
This led researchers to study how some parameters or rules change with respect to national cultures, for which it's possible to extract statical results.
However, even if this approach is the state of the art, it may approximate the concept of culture or may generalize too much individual preferences, related for example to other cultural parameters such as age and gender or others.

In general, the concept of culture is complex and fuzzy, and even if these approaches are considered valuables, we may need to introduce a different paradigm.
This project is devoted to understand whether a combination of previous knowledge and user-feedback adaptation help individuals to see Pepper robot as more culturally competent.

## The scenario
Pepper robot is a very famous social robots, capable of speaking and moving intelligently inside environments.
In our scenario, Pepper robot must go from a point A to a point B, while interacting verbally and non physically with a human in position C.
The robot should be able to adjust some parameters with respect to the culture of the user. 
The considered parameters are:
- proximity (distance from human to robot);
- language
- language style (high-context, low-context)

Other parameters could be introduced in the future, but we selected these ones because there is evidence in literature that they are mutually influenced by culture.
